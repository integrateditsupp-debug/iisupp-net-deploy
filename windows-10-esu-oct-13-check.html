What stops getting patched, and what it costs if you wait.

Three Microsoft support dates land on the same Tuesday: the first paid year of Windows 10 Extended Security Updates for organizations ends, Office 2021 reaches end of support, and the last Windows Server 2012 ESU year closes. What Microsoft's own pages say

Already happened · October 14, 2025
Windows 10 reached end of support

Microsoft's lifecycle page for Windows 10 Home and Pro:

“Windows 10 will reach end of support on October 14, 2025. The current version, 22H2, will be the final version of Windows 10, and all editions will remain in support with monthly security update releases through that date.”

What Microsoft stopped providing on that date, from its ESU page: technical support, feature updates or new features, and quality updates (including security and reliability fixes). Also from that page: “Windows 10 PCs will continue to work” — they keep running; they stop being patched.

Source: learn.microsoft.com/en-us/lifecycle/products/windows-10-home-and-pro · learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/whats-new/extended-security-updates
October 13, 2026
Windows 10 ESU for organizations — Year One ends, Year Two must be bought

Microsoft's lifecycle FAQ lists the three ESU years for Windows 10: Year 1 ends October 13, 2026, Year 2 ends October 12, 2027, Year 3 ends October 10, 2028. Eligible editions per that FAQ: “Enterprise, Education, and Pro in Commercial use”; devices must be running Windows 10, version 22H2.

“Extended Security Updates for organizations and businesses on Windows 10 can be purchased today through the Microsoft Volume Licensing Program, at $61 USD per device for Year One.” … “The price doubles every consecutive year, for a maximum of three years.” … “If you decide to purchase the program in Year Two, you have to pay for Year One too, as ESUs are cumulative.”

Honesty note: Microsoft does not print the Year-Two or Year-Three dollar figure on that page. It says the price doubles; the exact amount, in USD or CAD, is something to check on Microsoft's page or with your licensing reseller before you budget. ESU is sold by the year, not in partial periods. Microsoft also lists Windows 10 virtual machines on Windows 365 and several Azure services as eligible for ESU at no additional cost.

Source: learn.microsoft.com/en-us/lifecycle/faq/extended-security-updates · learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/whats-new/extended-security-updates
Consumer programme · runs to October 12, 2027 (as published 2026-10-09)
Windows 10 consumer ESU — and why it is not the answer for a business PC

Microsoft's consumer ESU page, as fetched on 2026-10-09, says you can enroll “any time until the program ends on October 12, 2027”, with three enrollment options: no additional cost if you are syncing your PC settings, 1,000 Microsoft Rewards points, or a one-time purchase of $30 USD; one licence covers up to 10 devices. It needs Windows 10 version 22H2 and a Microsoft account that is an administrator on the device.

“The consumer ESU program can’t be used in commercial scenarios, and enrollment won’t be offered for: Devices in kiosk mode. Devices joined to an Active Directory domain or Microsoft Entra and/or enrolled in a Mobile Device Management (MDM) solution. However, devices that are Microsoft Entra registered can use the Consumer ESU program.”

So a domain-joined or Intune-managed office PC is on the organization programme above, with its October 13, 2026 year boundary — not the $30 consumer one. If you were counting on the consumer option for a work fleet, that is the first thing to recheck.

Source: microsoft.com/windows/extended-security-updates
October 13, 2026
Office 2021 and Office LTSC 2021 reach end of support

Microsoft's lifecycle page for Office 2021 (Home and Business, Home and Business for Mac, Home and Student, Home and Student for Mac, Professional) shows a retirement date of 10/14/2026 6:59:59 AM Pacific Time — that is the end of October 13, 2026 in Redmond’s time zone. Office LTSC 2021 (Standard, Standard for Mac) shows the same mainstream end date. Neither page lists an Extended Security Update option.

Separately, Microsoft states that Microsoft 365 Apps (the subscription version) running on Windows 10 keep receiving security updates until October 10, 2028, and feature updates until Version 2608 is released — but with reduced support: if an issue occurs only on Windows 10, “support will ask the customer to move to Windows 11.”

Source: learn.microsoft.com/en-us/lifecycle/products/office-2021 · learn.microsoft.com/en-us/lifecycle/products/office-ltsc-2021 · learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-apps/end-of-support/windows-10-support
October 13, 2026
Windows Server 2012 and 2012 R2 — the last ESU year ends

Microsoft's lifecycle page for Windows Server 2012 shows extended support ending 10/11/2023 6:59:59 AM Pacific (the FAQ states it plainly: “Windows Server 2012 and 2012 R2 Extended Support ended on October 10, 2023”), followed by three Extended Security Update years. Year 3 runs 10/15/2025 8:00:00 AM to 10/14/2026 6:59:59 AM Pacific; the FAQ prints the Year 3 end as October 13, 2026. The FAQ describes ESU as security updates “up to three years after the product’s End of Extended Support date” — there is no fourth year listed.

After that date a 2012 server is on a 14-year-old operating system with no update programme behind it. The usual places one hides: an old file server, a domain controller nobody wanted to touch, the box a line-of-business application was installed on in 2014, or a virtual machine that was “going to be decommissioned”.

Source: learn.microsoft.com/en-us/lifecycle/products/windows-server-2012 · learn.microsoft.com/en-us/lifecycle/faq/extended-security-updates

What to do this week

  1. Count. Every Windows 10 PC (Settings → System → About, or run winver), every Office 2021 install (File → Account in Word shows the product name), every server still on Windows Server 2012 or 2012 R2 — including virtual machines and the ones nobody logs into. A list with a name against each row is the whole first step; most businesses have never written it down.
  2. Split the Windows 10 list into “can upgrade” and “cannot”. Microsoft’s PC Health Check app reports whether a PC meets Windows 11 requirements. The ones that can: schedule the upgrade. The ones that cannot: a replacement date or an ESU decision, nothing else is a plan.
  3. Decide ESU for the PCs you will still have after October 13, 2026. Organization ESU is bought by the year through volume licensing, it is cumulative, and Microsoft says the price doubles each year — get the Year-Two figure in writing from Microsoft’s page or your reseller before you commit. Personal devices that are not domain-joined or managed fall under the consumer programme, which Microsoft currently lists as running to October 12, 2027.
  4. Office 2021: pick the replacement now, not in October. Microsoft lists no ESU for Office 2021 or Office LTSC 2021. Move to a supported Office — Microsoft 365 Apps, or the current one-time-purchase release; check Microsoft’s lifecycle page for its dates — and note that Microsoft 365 Apps on a Windows 10 PC are themselves on a reduced-support footing.
  5. Windows Server 2012: treat it as a migration, with a date. Nothing on Microsoft’s page runs past October 13, 2026. Find out what actually depends on the box (shares, printers, the application, the domain role), then move it — to a current Windows Server, to the cloud service that replaced that function, or off entirely. A server that cannot be migrated by the date needs to be isolated from the internet and documented as a known risk, in writing, by someone with the authority to accept it.
  6. Put the date in the calendar with an owner and a budget line. October 13, 2026 is a Tuesday. If you carry cyber insurance or have compliance obligations, check whether your renewal questionnaire asks about unsupported software — it is cheaper to answer “none” than to explain.
  7. Keep the sources. The links on this page go to Microsoft’s pages, not ours. Recheck them before any purchase; a snapshot is only as good as the day it was taken, and we would rather you see Microsoft’s current page than trust ours.

Questions people ask us

Does Windows 10 stop working on October 13?

No. Microsoft’s own ESU page says “Windows 10 PCs will continue to work.” What changes is whether they receive security updates. A PC that was never enrolled in ESU stopped receiving them after October 14, 2025. A PC on organization ESU needs the next year purchased to keep receiving them after October 13, 2026. A PC on the consumer programme is, per Microsoft’s current page, covered to October 12, 2027.

Can I just put the $30 consumer ESU on our office PCs?

Microsoft says no for the common office set-up: the consumer programme “can’t be used in commercial scenarios”, and enrollment is not offered to devices joined to an Active Directory domain or Microsoft Entra, or enrolled in an MDM solution. Devices that are only Microsoft Entra registered can use it. If your PCs sign in with a company account and are managed, plan on the organization programme.

What does ESU actually include?

Microsoft’s page: ESU “enables PCs to continue to receive critical and important security updates through an annual subscription service after support ends.” It does not include new features, customer-requested non-security updates, design change requests, or technical support — “Microsoft will provide support for customers that encounter challenges related to the ESU” itself, nothing more. It is a bridge, not a destination.
