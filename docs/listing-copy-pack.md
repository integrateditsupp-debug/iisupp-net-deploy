# Integrated IT Support Inc. — Master Listing Copy Pack

One source of truth for every directory, portal, supplier register, and profile.
Copy from here. Never retype from memory — inconsistent name/address/phone (NAP) data
is the single fastest way to tank local search ranking and get a Google Business Profile
flagged.

Last updated: 2026-07-29

## 1. Canonical NAP block — use this exact text everywhere

```
Integrated IT Support Inc.
30 Fothergill Court
Whitby, Ontario  L1P 1L4
Canada
(647) 581-3182
https://iisupp.net
integrateditsupp@gmail.com
```

Rules that are not optional:

- Legal name is **Integrated IT Support Inc.** — with the period on "Inc." Do not list as
  "Integrated IT Support", "IIS", "IISupport", or "iisupp.net". Directories match on exact
  string; variants create duplicate entities.
- Postal code is **L1P 1L4**. Some older outbound templates carry **L1P 1L4** — that is
  wrong, and under CASL an inaccurate physical address in a commercial email is a defect.
  Fix it wherever it appears before it propagates into a public listing.
- Phone renders as **(647) 581-3182** in North American forms and **+1 647 581 3182**
  in international forms (UAE, Saudi, Qatar, UN). Same number, format matched to region.
- Website is always `https://iisupp.net` — with `https`, without `www`.

## 6. Hook variants — rotate these, do not reuse one everywhere

A hook is the line that earns the click. Directory listings that read like a services
menu get skimmed past. These lead with the buyer's problem, not our capability. Rotate
so the same sentence is not indexed on forty domains — duplicate copy across directories
is treated as low-quality by search engines and dilutes every listing.

**Hook 1 — the cost of the status quo**
> Most small businesses do not have an IT problem. They have a "nobody owns it" problem —
> until the server dies on a Friday. We own it, for a flat monthly fee.

**Hook 2 — the response-time promise**
> Your last IT company answered when they got around to it. Ours commits response times
> in writing, and you can hold us to them.

**Hook 3 — the lock-in objection**
> Month-to-month. No three-year contract, no exit fee. We would rather earn the renewal
> than enforce it.

**Hook 4 — the "one person knows everything" risk**
> If one person is the only one who knows how your network is set up, that is not an IT
> department — that is a single point of failure. We document everything and hand you the keys.

**Hook 5 — the budget-predictability angle**
> Flat monthly pricing. No surprise invoices after an outage, because preventing the
> outage is what you are already paying for.

## 8. Things to never put in a public listing

- Any street address other than the canonical one above. Virtual offices, coworking
  desks and mail drops violate Google Business Profile guidelines and risk suspension of
  the legitimate Whitby profile. This applies with force to any Gulf-region listing —
  do **not** create a UAE, Saudi or Qatar Google Business Profile without a real staffed
  premises there.
- Claims of certification, partner tier, clearance or designation we do not hold.
  Procurement registers cross-check, and a false designation is grounds for
  disqualification and debarment, not just correction.
- Indigenous, women-owned, veteran-owned or minority-owned business status unless it is
  factually true. The Indigenous Business Directory requires 51%+ Indigenous ownership.
- Any pricing figure. Directory pricing goes stale and becomes an anchor in negotiation.
  Say "flat monthly pricing" and take the conversation to a call.
- Placeholder data. The site currently contains at least two dummy phone numbers
  ((123) 456-7890 and (416) 555-0100) in template files — verify none of these leak into
  a listing form.
