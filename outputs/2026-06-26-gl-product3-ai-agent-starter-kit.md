## Chapter 1 — What AI Can Actually Do for a Small Business

### 1.1 The realistic picture

AI tools today are excellent at:
- Writing first drafts (emails, proposals, SOPs, FAQs)
- Summarizing long documents or email threads
- Answering questions from a knowledge base or document set
- Sorting and triaging incoming requests
- Generating checklists, templates, and structured notes

AI tools today are NOT reliable for:
- Making final decisions on money, legal, or compliance matters
- Replacing your judgment on customer relationships
- Fully autonomous action without a human review step
- Tasks that require real-time business data you have not connected

### 1.2 The three tiers of AI use for small business

**Tier 1 — Prompt tools (zero setup, start today)**  
Use ChatGPT, Copilot, or Gemini directly in a chat window. You type a request, it returns a draft. You review, edit, send or use. Best for: email drafts, meeting notes, proposal outlines, job posts.

**Tier 2 — Workflow automations (low setup, 1–2 hours)**  
Use Power Automate, Zapier, or Make to connect tools you already pay for. Trigger: new form submission → summarize with AI → email you the summary. Best for: intake forms, FAQ autoresponders, lead routing, file naming.

**Tier 3 — Custom AI assistants or agents (medium setup, 3–8 hours or hire IIS)**  
Build a simple AI assistant trained on your own documents or SOPs. Answers staff questions. Handles client intake. Routes support tickets. Best for: help desks, client onboarding, team knowledge bases.

| Question | If YES → use case |
|---|---|

### 2.2 Scoring your use case for quick wins

Pick the use case that scores highest:

- **Frequency:** Happens daily = 3 pts, weekly = 2 pts, monthly = 1 pt
- **Time lost:** >1 hr = 3 pts, 30 min = 2 pts, <30 min = 1 pt
- **Staff involved:** 3+ people = 3 pts, 2 people = 2 pts, just you = 1 pt
- **Data availability:** You have the source content = 3 pts, needs building = 1 pt

Highest score = start here.

## Chapter 3 — Setting Up Your First AI Workflow

### 3.1 Use case: Email draft assistant (Tier 1)

**Tool:** ChatGPT (free) or Microsoft Copilot (M365)  
**Setup time:** 15 minutes  

**Step 1 — Write your master prompt template**

Save this in a text file named `email-prompt.txt` and keep it bookmarked:

```
You are a professional assistant for [Your Business Name]. 
We are a [type of business] serving [target client type].
Our tone is [professional / friendly / direct].

Write a [type: follow-up / proposal / intro / complaint response / quote] email to [recipient name] at [company].
Context: [paste context here]
Keep it under 150 words unless the topic requires more.
Do not make up specific numbers, dates, or promises.
End with a clear next step.
```

**Step 2 — Run it for 5 days**  
Fill in the brackets each time, paste into ChatGPT or Copilot, review and edit the output, then send from your own email. Log how long it saves you.

**Step 3 — Refine the prompt**  
After 5 days, note what consistently needs editing. Add that to your master prompt. Your prompt gets better over time.

---

### 3.2 Use case: FAQ autoresponder (Tier 2)

**Tool:** Microsoft Power Automate or Zapier + any email system  
**Setup time:** 1–2 hours  

**Step 1 — List your top 10 customer questions**  
Pull from your inbox. Identify the 10 questions you answer the same way every time.

**Step 2 — Write your FAQ document**  
Format: one question per section. Plain English. Under 100 words per answer. Save as a Word doc or Google Doc.

**Step 3 — Build the trigger flow**  
In Power Automate or Zapier:
- Trigger: new email arrives with keyword (e.g. "price", "hours", "how do I")
- Action: send auto-reply with FAQ answer linked
- Optional: flag for human review before sending

**Step 4 — Monitor for 2 weeks**  
Log: how many FAQs were auto-answered, what errors came up, what new questions appeared.

---

### 3.3 Use case: AI knowledge assistant for staff (Tier 3)

**Tool:** Microsoft Copilot Studio (M365) or ChatGPT custom GPT (Plus tier) or **IIS implementation**  
**Setup time:** 3–8 hours (or book a call with IIS)  

**Step 1 — Build your knowledge base**  
Gather: your top SOPs, FAQ doc, product/service descriptions, common email replies, escalation rules. Save all as clean text files or Word docs. Remove any personal client data.

**Step 2 — Upload to your AI tool**  
In Copilot Studio or a custom GPT: upload the documents. Set the system instruction to "Answer staff questions about our procedures using only the uploaded documents. If unsure, say 'I don't know — check with the manager'."

**Step 3 — Test with 20 real questions**  
Have one staff member ask 20 questions they would normally ask the manager. Score: correct, partially correct, wrong. Anything wrong — check the source document. Fix the doc, not the AI.

**Step 4 — Roll out to team with guardrails**  
Tell staff: "This assistant helps with standard procedures only. For anything involving client money, legal, or disputes — come to me directly."

## Chapter 4 — Prompts You Can Use Today

Copy and adapt these. Test each in ChatGPT or Copilot before using in production.

### Email drafts
```
Write a professional follow-up email to [Name] at [Company]. 
Context: [paste notes from call or meeting]. 
Goal: [confirm next step / schedule meeting / send proposal]. 
Tone: [direct / warm / formal]. Under 120 words.
```

### Quote/proposal intro
```
Write a short proposal intro for [service name] to [client type].
Their main problem: [describe in 1 sentence].
Our solution: [describe in 1 sentence].
Keep it 3 short paragraphs. No jargon.
```

### Meeting summary
```
Summarize this meeting transcript into:
- 3 key decisions
- Action items (who + what + by when)
- Open questions
Format as bullet points. No filler.
[Paste transcript or notes below]
```

### SOP draft
```
Write a step-by-step SOP for [process name].
Audience: [staff role].
Frequency: [how often this happens].
Goal: [what a perfect completion looks like].
Include: 5–8 steps, a checklist at the end, and one common error to avoid.
```

### Customer complaint response
```
Write a professional and empathetic reply to this customer complaint.
Complaint: [paste complaint text].
Our policy on this issue: [describe briefly].
Do not offer refunds or credits unless the policy allows it.
End with a clear next step and a contact option.
```

## Chapter 5 — Guardrails and Team Rules

### 5.1 What your team must never do with AI

Post this where your team can see it.

1. **Never send AI output without reading it first.** AI makes confident-sounding errors.
2. **Never paste client personal data into a public AI tool.** Use your private M365 Copilot or a data-safe system.
3. **Never use AI to make final decisions on money, legal, or compliance.**
4. **Never claim AI output is human-written when the client expects human judgment.**
5. **Always tell the AI what NOT to do** (e.g. "do not promise a specific delivery date").

### 5.2 Your minimum AI policy (copy and save)

```
[Business Name] AI Use Policy — Version 1.0

We use AI tools to draft, summarize, and assist staff.

Rules:
- All AI output is reviewed by a human before use.
- No client personal data is pasted into public AI tools.
- AI does not make final decisions on financial, legal, or regulatory matters.
- Staff unsure about AI output escalate to [manager name].
- This policy is reviewed every 6 months.

Approved tools: [list your tools here]
Owner: [your name]
Date: [date]
```
