## Why Offshore Now

IIS is one person. The highest-leverage expansion move is not hiring full-time locally — it's building a hybrid delivery model where Ahmad handles business development, escalations, and client relationships while an offshore L1/L2 tier handles ticket volume, monitoring, and documentation.

Benefits:
- 24/7 coverage without 24/7 personal effort.
- Competitive pricing (can undercut local MSPs by 20–40%).
- Scale to more clients without proportional cost increase.
- Positions IIS for larger contracts that require coverage SLAs.

---

## Delivery Model Options

### Option A: Offshore Talent Pool (Solo Contractors)

- Source: Upwork, Toptal, OnlineJobs.ph, LinkedIn (Philippines, India, Pakistan, Eastern Europe).
- Relationship: direct contractor agreements.
- Control: high. Ahmad sets standards, reviews tickets.
- Cost: $8–20/hr for L1; $15–35/hr for L2 with specific certs (CompTIA, Microsoft).
- Risk: contractor quality varies; vetting is Ahmad's responsibility; availability not guaranteed.
- Best for: pilot phase. Start with 1–2 contractors on hourly basis for overflow only.

### Option B: Offshore BPO / Helpdesk Vendor Partnership

- Examples: Cloudstaff, Acquire BPO, TaskUs (Philippines); SupportYourApp (Ukraine/Romania).
- Relationship: B2B contract with BPO who manages the workforce.
- Control: medium. SLA-based, not task-level.
- Cost: $12–22/hr all-in; minimum seat commitment (typically 1–3 FTE).
- Risk: minimum commitment before IIS has client volume to fill it; less flexibility.
- Best for: when IIS has 3+ MSP clients needing coverage.

### Option C: Reseller / Subcontract with Existing IT Staffing Firm

- Source: Canadian IT staffing firm that has offshore capacity already.
- Relationship: IIS acts as front-end; staffing firm provides back-end offshore support.
- Control: lowest — IIS is the account manager, not the operator.
- Risk: margin compression; quality not fully controlled.
- Best for: landing large contracts IIS cannot staff alone.

**Recommended path for Ahmad now:** Option A pilot (1 contractor, overflow-only) → Option B when 3+ clients confirmed.

## Minimum Viable Setup

Before the first offshore hire:

1. **Ticketing system:** Freshdesk (free tier) or HubSpot Service Hub — must be accessible by offshore team.
2. **Knowledge base:** IIS KB must have 20+ articles (M365 KB Pack is the starting point) so contractors can self-serve answers.
3. **Escalation protocol:** clear decision tree — what the offshore tech resolves vs. what escalates to Ahmad (email + 4-hour SLA).
4. **Security baseline:** contractor signs NDA + AUP; uses IIS-provided VPN or limited-access environment; no direct access to client admin credentials.
5. **Daily summary:** offshore tech writes a ticket summary each shift end — Ahmad reviews async.

## Risk Flags

- **Data residency / privacy:** Canadian client data handled by offshore team = potential PIPEDA concern. Mitigation: no raw PII in ticketing notes; contractor operates in read-only/masked data mode.
- **Client perception:** some SMBs prefer Canadian-only support. Mitigation: IIS is the relationship; offshore is backend; only disclose if asked.
- **Quality control:** offshore contractor mishandling a ticket could damage a client relationship. Mitigation: all tickets above L1 require Ahmad review before close for first 60 days.
- **Contractor dependency:** if sole contractor goes offline, SLA breaks. Mitigation: always maintain 2 contractors or clear escalation to Ahmad.
