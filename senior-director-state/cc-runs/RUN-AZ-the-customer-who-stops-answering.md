## Why this sequence

RUN-AY opened the conversation that starts after money moves and got three days into it: what a
paying customer receives, what we promised them and how fast, and what their first week costs.

It stopped at the end of week one. Everything after that is unexamined, and the thing that kills a
one-founder MSP is not the first week — it is month four, when a customer who was happy stops
answering and nobody notices until the renewal does not happen.

Three things are true and none of them have been checked:

**Nothing in this repository can tell whether a customer has gone quiet.** AY2 proved the response
times we PROMISE disagree across surfaces. It never asked the prior question: is there anything on
this machine that could tell us a promise was missed, or that a customer who wrote every week has
written nothing in three? A commitment nobody can measure is a commitment nobody can keep, and this
one is measured by a person remembering.

**The renewal is an artefact nobody has looked for.** The pilot agreement, the MSA and the plan table
all describe a term. Whether anything in the shared line tells a customer — or us — when that term
ends, what happens on the day it does, and what a renewal conversation is even supposed to contain,
has never been asked. AT and AU built the path INTO the contract. Nothing has ever walked out of one.

**The second sale has never been named.** Every cycle since RUN-M has been about the first dollar. A
multi-million-dollar services company is not built on first dollars; it is built on the same customer
paying more next year than this year. What we would sell a happy customer at month six exists nowhere
— not as a document, not as a price, not as a trigger.

Same shape as every finding this series has produced: not a wrong answer, an unasked question.
