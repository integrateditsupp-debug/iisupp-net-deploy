# Office Move / Property IT Readiness

Updated: 2026-06-11T07:45:00-04:00

## What it is
- A scoped move-in and office-readiness offer for teams that need technology, user setup, vendor coordination, and day-one support handled more cleanly.
- Built for buyers who do not need a full MSP pitch, but do need someone to organize the messy IT details before, during, or right after a move, expansion, or workspace refresh.

## Best buyers
- Property and facilities leaders coordinating tenant or office readiness
- Operations leaders handling relocation, expansion, or hybrid-space changes
- Office managers responsible for day-one setup, onboarding, and room readiness
- Internal IT teams stretched across a move, refresh, or multi-vendor handoff
- Project teams that need practical support coordination without pretending IIS is the construction prime

## Problems it solves
- IT details get planned too late and day-one readiness turns chaotic
- Microsoft 365 access, laptops, printers, meeting rooms, Wi-Fi, and onboarding steps are spread across too many owners
- Vendor coordination is messy and the business is not sure what must be confirmed before people arrive
- Support demand spikes after a move or expansion and nobody wants senior staff firefighting basic issues all day

## IIS outcome
- Move-in IT checklist tied to the actual workspace and user needs
- Vendor coordination tracker for technology-related dependencies and open questions
- Day-one support and escalation plan with approval boundaries
- Clear list of what IIS can help coordinate versus what must stay with the landlord, cabling vendor, builder, or client approver
- Post-move support observations and recurring-issue shortlist if the buyer wants follow-on help

## Suggested price posture
- Readiness review / planning pass: **$500-$1,500**
- Scoped move-in IT readiness sprint: **$2,500-$8,500**
- Day-one support or follow-on stabilization: **custom after scope**

Do not publish final pricing without Ahmad approval.

## Safe scope language
- move-in checklist and readiness review
- Microsoft 365 onboarding, account, device, and support readiness
- printer, workstation, and meeting-room coordination checklist
- vendor handoff tracker and escalation plan
- day-one support planning and post-move issue triage
- scoped coordination support, not general contracting

## What IIS should not claim without proof
- electrical, structured cabling, or low-voltage prime responsibility
- landlord-approved building access control authority
- 24/7 onsite staffing or national field-service scale
- construction-management ownership
- unsupported claims about certifications, permits, or large move crews

## Delivery steps
1. Review the move, opening, refresh, or expansion timeline.
2. Confirm users, rooms, devices, vendors, and approval boundaries.
3. Build the checklist for accounts, devices, network, meeting rooms, printers, and support handoff.
4. Track open dependencies and who owns each answer.
5. Prepare day-one support coverage and escalation notes.
6. Deliver a short post-move issue view and recommendations if follow-on help is needed.
