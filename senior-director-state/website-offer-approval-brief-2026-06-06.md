## Objective

Make the next revenue step easy to approve so Codex can move from planning into reversible build work without guessing.

## Recommended Decisions

### 1. Pricing posture

Recommendation:
- Do not publish prices yet.
- Use CTA language that offers a short review, readiness review, or checklist.
- Keep price ranges internal until one or two real sales conversations validate buyer comfort.

Why:
- This protects margin while IIS is still refining packaging.
- It avoids anchoring buyers before scope is understood.
- It keeps public pages simple and lower-risk while proof language is still being approved.

What Codex can do immediately after approval:
- Build the three service pages without pricing blocks.
- Use the same shared CTA pattern across all pages.

What stays blocked:
- Public "starting at" or fixed package prices.
- Any quote-like pricing language on the website.

### 2. Proof / case-study posture

Recommendation:
- Use anonymized proof language only for now.
- Describe outcomes and deliverables without naming clients, vertical-sensitive facts, or unapproved references.

Why:
- IIS has usable capability language but no approved named reference pack for public use yet.
- Anonymous proof is enough to support service pages without creating claim risk.
- This is consistent with the current public-safe capability statement.

Approved style example:
- "Senior-led Microsoft 365 cleanup and AI-readiness roadmap for a professional-services team."

Avoid until separately approved:
- Named client logos.
- Named testimonials.
- Claimed percentages, savings, or timelines presented as guaranteed results.

What Codex can do immediately after approval:
- Add short anonymous proof callouts or FAQ answers to the service pages.

### 3. CTA destination

Recommendation:
- Route the primary CTA to a simple contact/intake form.
- Route the secondary CTA to ARIA chat for low-friction exploration.

Why:
- A form is better for capturing scope, buyer type, and follow-up context.
- ARIA chat is useful as a softer secondary path without forcing email outreach.
- This splits "ready to talk" visitors from "still exploring" visitors cleanly.

Recommended pattern by page:
- Primary CTA: request review via form.
- Secondary CTA: ask ARIA / view checklist / see what is included.

Do not recommend as primary CTA yet:
- Direct email link only.
- Booking form, unless Ahmad wants calendar-driven lead handling.

What Codex can do immediately after approval:
- Build one reusable intake pattern for all three service pages.
- Keep ARIA as a secondary conversion path only.

### 4. Outreach posture

Recommendation:
- Keep all SMB drafts no-send.
- Approve outreach only after the service pages or intake path exist.

Why:
- Sending before the website/intake flow is ready creates avoidable leakage.
- The drafts are better once CTA destination and proof language are locked.

What Codex can do immediately after approval:
- Nothing external.
- Optionally convert the best draft segments into public-safe page language and intake field presets.

## One-Page Decision Table

| Decision | Options | Recommendation | Revenue effect | Risk effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Website pricing | None / ranges / fixed prices | None for now | Slightly slower top-of-funnel, stronger margin control | Low |
| Proof style | Named / anonymized | Anonymized | Good enough to convert early buyers | Low |
| Primary CTA | Form / ARIA / email / booking | Form | Better qualification and follow-up context | Low |
| Secondary CTA | ARIA / checklist / email | ARIA or checklist | Helps exploratory visitors convert softly | Low |
| Outreach timing | Send now / hold | Hold until intake path is live | Better downstream conversion quality | Low |

## Fastest Safe Path

If Ahmad wants the least-risk, fastest-progress path:

1. Approve no public prices.
2. Approve anonymized proof only.
3. Approve form-first CTA with ARIA as secondary.
4. Keep outreach unsent until the pages or shared intake path are live.

Result:
- Codex can build and locally QA the three service pages immediately after approval.
- No external outreach, publishing, pricing, or irreversible commitment is required in the approval itself.

## What Remains Blocked Even After This Brief

- Any external outreach to prospects or partners.
- Any public deploy of the new service pages.
- Any price publication, quote, or estimate unless Ahmad explicitly approves that posture.
- Any named client/reference use.
