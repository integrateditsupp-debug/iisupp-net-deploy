## Trend Opportunity Score

Use a 0-100 score with explanations.

Suggested formula:

`Trend Opportunity Score = Attention + Velocity + Longevity + Usefulness + Revenue Potential + IIS Fit + ARIA Fit + Growth Library Fit + Trust Potential - Legal Risk - Ethical Risk - Saturation Risk - Build Difficulty - Cost Risk`

Default deterministic weights until changed by a reviewed spec:

- Attention: 10
- Velocity: 10
- Longevity: 10
- Usefulness: 12
- Revenue Potential: 12
- IIS Fit: 10
- ARIA Fit: 10
- Growth Library Fit: 8
- Trust Potential: 8
- Distribution Fit: 5
- Defensibility: 5
- Legal Risk penalty: up to -10
- Ethical Risk penalty: up to -10
- Saturation Risk penalty: up to -8
- Build Difficulty penalty: up to -7
- Cost Risk penalty: up to -5

Rules:
- Scoring must be deterministic for the same input; do not let an LLM silently invent a score.
- Store the score breakdown, not only the total.
- If real source data is missing, mark the score as `estimated` and explain the missing evidence.

Each score must explain:
- why users care
- whether the trend is hype, useful, or structural
- what IIS can build from it
- what ARIA can learn from it
- what Growth Library product or demo can be created
- what should be avoided
- whether Ahmad approval is needed

## Longevity Classifier

Classify every trend with one of:

- Flash: likely days to weeks; use for quick content only.
- Seasonal: tied to a calendar, event, school, tax, budget, holiday, or buying cycle.
- Medium: useful for 1-6 months; candidate for a small paid guide or demo.
- Long: useful for 6-24 months; candidate for Growth Library product and ARIA KB.
- Structural-Shift: durable behavior/market change; candidate for IIS service, ARIA product, and command-center tracking.

Use rule-based classification first. LLM interpretation can explain the class but should not override the rule without a review note.

- `round_id`
- `owner`
- `objective`
- `context read`
- `what changed`
- `evidence`
- `next best task`
- `files to touch`
- `approval gates`

- `question for cowork`

1. Codex reads the latest queue, memory, command files, and cowork packet.
2. Codex executes safe file/code/product work to the CEO final-action point.
3. Codex writes a compact handoff packet with exact next questions/tasks for Claude.
4. Claude reviews strategy, market logic, offer quality, and risk posture.
5. Claude writes a sharper prompt or critique back into the queue.
6. Codex implements the next safe slice.
7. Repeat until the lane reaches a CEO final-action gate or a real blocker.
