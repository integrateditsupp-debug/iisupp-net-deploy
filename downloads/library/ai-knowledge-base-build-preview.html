Sample service shape

What a cleaner support-knowledge build leaves behind

Content model

Article template, symptom classes, audience split, safe-first checks, admin boundary, escalation signal, and versioning rules.

Source cleanup

Ticket review, SOP normalization, duplicate-pattern collapse, and a simple rule for what becomes canonical versus archive-only.

Operational handoff

Ownership model, update rhythm, QA checklist, and a practical first rollout plan so the KB stays usable instead of rotting after delivery.

The build stays scoped. It does not assume a platform rip-and-replace, connector rollout, or legal compliance posture. IIS structures the support brain first, then the buyer decides what should remain human-led or move into later tooling.
What the discovery prompt should answer

The questions that matter before a build

  • Where do repeated support answers live now?
  • Which issue types create the most ticket churn or inconsistent answers?
  • Which steps must remain technician-only or approval-gated?
  • What does a trustworthy first KB milestone look like?
  • Which teams or users need the cleaner structure first?
