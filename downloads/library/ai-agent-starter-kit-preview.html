What this preview proves

The value is not "AI agents everywhere." The value is choosing one workflow that is repetitive enough to matter, safe enough to pilot, and structured enough to review without creating silent risk.

The preview keeps the trust layer clear: no false autonomous-send claims, no hidden data-movement promises, and no assumption that private customer, HR, finance, or legal work should be delegated first.

The live build still depends on the buyer's process, tools, data sensitivity, and approval posture.

Inside the kit

What the buyer actually gets

  • A safe-first workflow filter for deciding whether a task should stay human, become AI-assisted, or move into a later-stage build.
  • A short list of practical first agent patterns: intake cleanup, FAQ response prep, follow-up drafting, task extraction, and knowledge lookup support.
  • Approval-gate language for work that still needs human review before anything is sent, committed, or changed in a system.
  • A simple ladder into an AI Workflow Audit or a Quick-Win Sprint when the buyer wants the first workflow mapped and staged for real use.
Best fit

Who should start here

  • Small businesses repeating the same intake, follow-up, FAQ, or document-prep work every week.
  • Operators and admins who want one useful AI step before they commit to a broader automation project.
  • Teams experimenting with AI already but lacking clear workflow boundaries, risk filters, and approval rules.
  • Leaders who need a calmer first map before they let a tool touch customer communication or internal process flow.
Sample section

How IIS scores a first AI-agent use case

This is a representative starter screen. The live pack expands it, but the point stays the same: do not start with the most sensitive workflow. Start with the most repetitive workflow that still tolerates review.

Good first fit

FAQ cleanup, intake summarization, meeting action extraction, recurring task prep, internal knowledge lookup, and owner-ready draft responses with human review before send.

Needs tighter controls

Quote support, customer follow-up drafting, hiring-screen support, finance summaries, or workflow updates that touch multiple systems and still need approval boundaries.

Do later or keep human-led

Anything involving final legal wording, payroll, disciplinary action, health data, bank movement, irrevocable commitments, or autonomous external sending without review.

If the workflow touches regulated data, external commitments, or system write-back without a review checkpoint, the preview posture is to slow down, map the process properly, and keep a human approval step in the loop.
What stays scoped

Reserved for live discovery

  • The exact workflow volume, system access, and source documents already in use across the buyer's team.
  • Whether the first implementation should stay prompt-led, become tool-assisted, or move into a fuller ARIA-style support path.
  • Any authenticated integration, account creation, tool selection, or production permission boundary.
  • Detailed pricing, rollout support, and ongoing maintenance posture beyond the first mapped workflow.
Best next move

Use the right lane after the preview

  • Use the AI Workflow Audit when the buyer still needs help choosing the first workflow and ranking the real opportunity.
  • Use the AI Workflow Quick-Win Sprint when one repetitive process is already obvious and the team wants a first implementation step.
  • Use ARIA or a broader IIS implementation only after the first workflow and review posture are clear enough to support safely.
